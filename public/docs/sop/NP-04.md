# Chapter 1: SSHQE Internal Audit {#np-04-1-sshqe-internal-audit}

## 1. Purpose {#np-04-1-1-purpose}

Element 10 of the SSHQE Management System states that a process which assesses performance relative to expectations is essential to improving performance. It thus requires a system be in place to periodically conduct assessments which determine the overall degree of conformance with expectations within the SSHQE Management System and that the workforce be actively involved in periodic self-assessments of the effectiveness of processes and procedures to meet the SSHQE expectations. Additionally, international standards for quality and for the environment (ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001), which PT. LNG has chosen to follow, require periodic third party and regular internal audits.

Given these expectations above, the purpose of this procedure is to:
- Establish the framework for conducting periodic internal SSHQE audits and how they yield a quantitative measure of conformance to the SSHQE Management System
- Describe how PT. LNG employees are involved in this process
- Specify the responsibilities and the methodology for audits related to the planning, scheduling, preparing, executing, reporting, action tracking and record keeping
- Standardize certain aspects of audits such as the Terms of Reference (TOR), Action Tracking and reporting formats such that the audit team can focus on content rather than format
- Evaluate the implementation of the PT. LNG Quality Management System (QMS) and the PT. LNG Environmental Management System (EMS) in order to assure these systems have been implemented and are in compliance with legal and other requirements and are implemented according to their respective management system plans.

## 2. Scope {#np-04-1-2-scope}

### In Scope
For clarity of the scope, it is beneficial to identify four distinct levels of auditing activities within PT. LNG:

### Table 1. Types of Audits {#np-04-1-2-types-of-audits}

| Level | Examples | Anticipated Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Third Party Audit | Government Agency, Certification Audits, Independent External HSE Audits, etc. | 1/year |
| 2. Stakeholder Audit | Audit by Co-owner, Audit by Customer, Audits requested by the Board of Directors | 1/year |
| 3. **Internal** SSHQE Audit | Management System Compliance Audit such as PSM Audit, ISO 9001/ ISO 14001 / ISO 45001 / SMK3 Audit, etc. | Minimum 1 (once)/year |
| 4. **Internal** Frequent SSHQE Audit | PTW Audit, CSMS Audit, etc. | Dozens/year |

This procedure applies to Type 3 Audits: Internal SSHQE audits. These audits serve to assure compliance with an internal SSHQE procedure, a regulatory requirement or an adopted standard.

Regarding Type 1 & Type 2 audits: the third parties that manage those audits must follow their own established procedures for conducting Level 1 and Level 2 audits. These audits are usually initiated by the third party but can be initiated by PT. LNG personnel upon recognition of a desire to assure compliance.

Regarding Type 4 Internal Frequent Audits: these are less formal than audits and typically rely on individual knowledge of specialists or utilize checklist to identify to any audit of PT. LNG activities. They are not required to follow this procedure. Leaders of those audits will determine the procedures for their inspections and reviews.

Third parties, stakeholders and other directorates and departments may adopt all or portions of this procedure as appropriate to fit their own purposes.

## 3. Definitions {#np-04-1-3-definitions}

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Audit – Internal or External | A structured process conducted to test and determine the degree of compliance with regard to applicable international agreements and standards; federal, provincial and local laws and regulations; and/or company policies and procedures. The goal of an audit is to express an opinion regarding compliance of an organization or system to a specified standard based on objective evidence. |
| Audit Criteria | Set of policies, procedures, plans or other specified requirements |

| Internal Management System Audit | A systematic and documented verification process carried out by the organization itself to obtain and to objectively evaluate evidences in order to determine whether the management system (EMS or QMS for example) of the organization has conformed to the audit criteria of the established management system. ### General {#np-04-1-4-general}

Auditing is a strategic business tool that is applied to assure effective management control. The general purpose of any audit is to provide assurance to management that the organization is functioning in accordance with requirements – and more important, how it can function more effectively. The findings from audits allow for early intervention and the application of corrective actions and improvement in order to enable PT. LNG to meets its mission and targets.

Audits have many facets; they can be internal or external and can focus on system, compliance or product aspects. These facets are explained below in Tables 3 and 4 below. These facets shall be identified in the Terms of Reference prior to initiating an audit so that the purpose is clear for all involved.

### Table 3. Comparison of Internal & External Audits {#np-04-1-4-comparison-internal-external-audits}

| Aspect | External Audits | Internal Audits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Report goes to… | Shareholders or agencies Outside the PT. LNG governance structure. | The Board of Directors and senior management who are within the organization’s governance structure. |
| Objective is to … | Add credibility and reliability to reports from the organization to its stakeholders outside agencies by giving opinion on the report | Evaluate and improve the effectiveness of governance, risk management and control processes. This provides members of the BOD and senior management with assurance that helps them fulfill their duties to the organization and its stakeholders. |
| Scope of the audit covers… | SSHQE activities requirements, reports, and risks. | All categories of risk, their management, including reporting on them. |
| Responsibility for improvement | Meet regulatory requirements and meet the duty to report violations. | Improvement is fundamental to the purpose of internal auditing. But it is done by advising, coaching and facilitating in order to not undermine the responsibility of management. |

The subjects of internal audits can be any combination of elements of the SSHQE Management System for any PT. LNG organization, department, group or team. External audits may be directed to Contractors, Vendors, Subcontractors, Shareholders, government agencies, independent certification agency or other third parties.

### Table 4. Comparison of Audit Aspects {#np-04-1-4-comparison-audit-aspects}

| Audit Aspect | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| System Audit | Measures conformance to a set of management system expectations. A system audit is an audit of a system or subsystem against system requirements. It can reveal conformity or Nonconformity to the system. |
| Compliance Audit | Measures compliance to a specific standard, procedure, Standing Order, regulation, etc. A compliance audit is an audit of individual administrative processes against predetermined steps or activities. It can reveal inefficiencies and areas for improvement. |
| Product Audit | Measures compliance of a manufactured product against an agreed-to standard for that product. |

The methodologies of the different types of audits vary from formal (e.g. quality system audit) to more generic. This procedure intends to standardize for the operation certain methodology aspects such as the Terms of Reference (TOR) and reporting formats. This will apply stronger to the formal audits than to the ones of a more generic nature. Generic means that the audit team members will determine the methodology to a large extent themselves in order to ensure a “Fit for Purpose” philosophy.

### Audit Plan {#np-04-1-4-audit-plan}

The SSHQE Audit Plan is maintained by the SSHQE Department – see the format in Appendix 1. This Audit Plan is reviewed and adjusted regularly by the SSHQE Senior Manager. The current version is maintained by the SSHQE Performance Tracking and Analyst.

### Scheduling Considerations – General {#np-04-1-4-scheduling-considerations-general}

The SSHQE Audit Plan provides details of the anticipated internal and external SSHQE audits. The schedule for SSHQE audits is based on following considerations:
- SSHQE risks present in the workplace as identified by the Corporate Risk Register
- Incidents that occur in the workplace that indicate specific areas for improvement in SSHQE performance
- SSHQE audits specified by procedural or regulatory requirements
- Level 1 Audits to achieve/maintain ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 certifications and expressed requirements of government agencies
- Level 2 audits requested by Stakeholders and the Board of Directors

### Level 1 Third Party Audits {#np-04-1-4-level-1-third-party-audits}

The frequency of External Audits will vary dependent upon the authority that is conducting the audit. PT. LNG will work with this authority to establish the frequency of these audits.

### ISO 9001, ISO 14001 AND ISO 45001 Audits {#np-04-1-4-iso-audits}

- Certification audits shall occur every 3 (three) years
- Internal QMS & EMS audits shall occur at least once a year
- Additional internal audits may be conducted outside the determined audit program on critical departments or as required/requested

### Audit Team {#np-04-1-4-audit-team}

The SSHQE Senior Manager shall designate the Audit Team Leader / Lead Auditor who will in turn prepare the Audit implementation and select the audit team. The team may involve external third-party specialist as well as PT. LNG staffs – depending on the scope of the audit, availability of PT. LNG personnel, and the judgment of the Team Leader (Lead Auditor).

The qualifications of the individual team members are paramount. First and foremost an audit must understand the technical aspects of the items being audited. Technical Knowledge shall include:
- SSHQE regulations and PT. LNG procedures
- SSHQE practices and industry standards
- Facility operations
- PT. LNG SSHQE management system

Furthermore, a competent auditor will excel in these personal attributes:
- Conducting interviews
- Recognizing and absorbing Objective Evidence
- Documenting the audit (work papers)
- Writing audit findings
- Gathering audit data
- Using sampling strategies
- Communicating audit results

Additionally, auditors performing ISO management system audits shall have attended and successfully passed an ISO 9001 / 14001 / 45001 internal audit training course within the past 3 years.

Auditor Team Leader / Lead Auditor is required to have attended and passed ISO 9001 / 14001 / 45001 Lead Auditor/Assessor training program or be registered in national or international auditor institutions.

### Terms of Reference & Planning the Audit {#np-04-1-4-tor-planning-audit}

Each audit performed under this procedure shall have an approved Terms of Reference document which describes details related to:
- Why this audit is being performed
- Identify the criteria for the audit and what standards will be used to measure conformance to requirements
- Who is responsible for execution of the audit
- Define the scope for the audit

The assigned Audit Team Leader is responsible for completing the Terms of Reference for an audit and obtaining approval from the designated authorities. Appendix 2 provides a format and example information to be in the Audit Schedule. When preparing the Terms of Reference the results of any previous relevant audit or review and corrective actions should be reviewed and be taken into account.

As part of preparing the Audit Schedule it is normal for the Team Leader to conduct the following Pre-Audit Activities:
- Select team members and assign audit responsibilities
- Gather and distribute background information
- Identify applicable federal, provincial, and local regulations and company policies and procedures
- Conduct advance visits to the facility (if necessary) to confirm specific aspects of the scope. These are referred to as Pre-Audit Inspections.
- Review and revise audit strategy and assigned duties to team members
- Determine and confirm arrangements with the team members and the facility, such as:
  - Travel arrangements
  - Hotel/travel reservations
- Prepare items for audit (forms, supplies, protocols).
- Coordinate pre-audit team meeting(s) as necessary.

Once the Audit Schedule has been approved the Audit Team Leader should gather or develop the information specified in the schedule. This is when:
- The audit team is recruited
- Initial commitments on personnel and schedules are made by management
- Protocols for the team shall be prepared to fit the schedule.
- Questions for interviews are prepared as necessary
- Information on the plant or process is collected as needed. This might include such documents as P&IDs, MSDS, process flow descriptions, plant layouts, etc. This information will be useful for training the auditors and during the various inspections for the audit. An audit notebook is useful for holding this information.

### Notification {#np-04-1-4-notification}

The Team Leader is responsible to notify the Auditee of the planned audit and providing him/her with the schedule. The initial contact – by phone or email – is aimed at establishing the main parameters of the audit. This includes discussion of the schedule, subject of the audit, determining a mutually acceptable date and duration, location, planned team members, availability of staff for interviews, etc.

### Opening Meeting {#np-04-1-4-opening-meeting}

All SSHQE audits will officially begin with an Opening Meeting. The typical agenda for this meeting is provided in Appendix 3. The purpose of the opening meeting is for the team leader to make necessary introductions and to re-affirm the purpose and scope of the audit as defined in the Terms of Reference.

A secondary purpose of the Opening Meeting is to establish the detailed schedule for the audit. This schedule shall address interviews, document reviews, and inspection of equipment or facilities. The purpose of having an agreed-to schedule is to include auditee personnel in the audit process as appropriate and to avoid, where possible, disruption of ongoing operational activity. By their very nature all audits disrupt ongoing activities; however, these disruptions should be minimized as best possible by a cooperative effort on behalf of the Audit Team Leader and the Auditee. The schedule is usually a dynamic document that changes each day to fit the evolving needs of the Audit Team and the Auditee.

The attendees to the opening meeting should include key staffs of the auditee that are expected to be involved in the audit. All attendees shall sign an attendance list to be included in the audit record.

### Information Gathering {#np-04-1-4-information-gathering}

Information should be gathered from a wide range of sources so that the observations of the audit team will be built on a firm foundation. Depending on the scope of the audit, the audit teams should gather information by the following means:
- Tours and physical inspections of facilities, processes and equipment, and observations of routine and emergency operations and practices. If permitted, and considered beneficial by the audit team, photographs should be taken.
- Thorough review of issued regulatory applications and issued permits. Copies of key documents should be made for future reference.
- Reviews of facility maintenance and performance records and documents
- Review of incidents in the work place. Interviews with the investigators may help to understand the issues involved.
- Interviews of key personnel, and a representative sample of the facility's employee population. Notes should be taken to maintain a record of the items discussed.

### Protocols {#np-04-1-4-protocols}

The audit team should use written protocols in order to assure a structured approach to the audit. These protocols may include procedures, questionnaires and/or checklists. These documents should be prepared prior to the Opening Meeting. Each auditor must be familiar with the protocols to which they are assigned. It’s also useful to send the protocols to the Auditee so they know what to expect in terms of helping the audit team do their job. The purpose of the protocols is to identify the international treaties/agreements, federal, provincial, and local laws, regulations, and PT. LNG policies and procedures that apply to the facility. The protocols assure a thorough and consistent approach by the entire team.

Protocols for the team shall be prepared to fit the TOR:
- Protocols for PT. LNG procedures and management systems shall be based on the Approved version of the relevant documents.
- Protocols for ISO 9001 / 14001 / 45001 shall be based on ISO standards
- Protocols shall be sent to the Auditee for their information
- Each auditor shall be familiar with his/her assigned protocols
- Pre-existing or generic protocols may be used, but they must be brought up-to-date or modified so they will be applicable to the facility being audited.

### Interviews {#np-04-1-4-interviews}

Interviews are used in auditing to obtain information not evident from physical inspections or reviews of records and documents. Interviews provide a means to evaluate the effectiveness of a management system and the level of awareness by the employees and contractors. Depending on the scope of the audit, interviews should be conducted with key facility management and staff, other employee groups and, where applicable, contractors.

Interviews may be conducted using a previously prepared questionnaire or interview guide containing questions and subjects to be covered. This assures different auditors follow the same line of questioning. Relevant information from the interview process should be included in the audit work papers. Interviewees should be informed of the purpose of the audit, and how information obtained in the interview will be used. In general, the identity of interviewees should remain anonymous when findings are presented to facility management.

### Professional Standards of Independence & Objectivity {#np-04-1-4-professional-standards}

Auditors shall be independent from the facility they are auditing. See Definitions for Auditor Independence. The SSHQE Manager shall be consulted regarding any conflict with this requirement.

An auditor, believing that a potential conflict of interest could arise due to previous involvement or anticipated future involvement with a specific facility or project should bring this matter to the Audit Team Leader’s attention.

Auditors must be objective in their observations, their interviews, their inspections, and their approach for testing and sampling. This means that another auditor, with the same assignment, viewing the same information would reach the same general conclusions. Protocols and a formal listing of questions help assure objectivity is maintained throughout the audit.

Auditors must at all times base their findings on objective facts and must not be influenced by comments that cannot be verified. If at any time during the audit process, the Team Leader has reason to believe that essential information is being withheld or intentionally withheld, the SSHQE Manager should be notified immediately.

The Audit Team Leader shall periodically review the work of each team member to verify that professional standards are being met and that appropriate data gathering and analytical techniques are being utilized by each team member. The SSHQE Manager shall randomly review the work of audit teams under his/her supervision to assure that professional standards have been maintained.

### Observations, Findings, Action Items {#np-04-1-4-observations-findings-action-items}

The Audit Team shall draw conclusions from their Information Gathering efforts. These are divided into three categories:
- Observations – Positive behaviors, practices and results or simply statements of fact
- Findings/Non-Conformance – Areas for improvement. Each Finding addresses a single topic of narrow scope.
- Action Items – Measures to correct Findings/Non-Conformances. The Action Items are provided in order to improve compliance with standards. See the Action Tracking Procedure for how to record and track Findings from audits. For ISO 9001 / 14001 / 45001 audits the Findings are referred to as non-conformances. See these referenced procedures for details on reporting and tracking QMS/EMS CAR

Conclusions by the Audit Team must be factual, objective, supported by proper documentation, and validated by the Audit Team Leader / Lead Auditor. The process of identifying these conclusions actually begins immediately after the Opening Meeting when the Information Gathering is initiated. The Audit Team Leader is responsible for meeting frequently with the team in order to guide the investigation and to clearly define and document the conclusions. It is common for the listing of conclusions to be modified as the audit progresses – evolving as the evidence is collected.

Action Items shall be officially provided to the auditee during the closing meeting and documented in the audit report. However, it is common practice to review the Findings with the Auditee as soon as they are written and as the audit progresses to assure:
- Findings are clearly written
- The audit team is doing a thorough job
- No evidence has been missed or misunderstood
- Action Items are appropriate and will be effective

### Closing Meeting {#np-04-1-4-closing-meeting}

The purpose of the Closing Meeting is to summarize the conclusions of the audit and present the Findings and Action Items. See Appendix 4 for a generic agenda for the Closing Meeting. The primary agenda item are the Findings and the Action Items. This meeting can be very short if these items have been accepted by the Auditee prior to the meeting. The meeting will be much longer if the Auditee does not agree with the Findings. It may be appropriate to adjourn the meeting and discuss these issues with only the key people involved until agreement has been reached between the Audit Team Leader and the Auditee.

### Audit Report {#np-04-1-4-audit-report}

The Audit Team Leader is responsible for documenting the results of the audit. He/She can delegate various duties to team members in order to accomplish this as soon as reasonably practicable. The Audit Report shall capture the activities and conclusions of the audit. The contents of the report shall, as a minimum, include:
- Executive Summary Background
- Audit Team
- Conclusions
- Positive Observations
- Areas for Improvement/Findings
- Action Items
- Attachments
  - Approved Terms of Reference
  - Evidence – photographs, documents, references
  - Opening Meeting – Agenda & Attendees
  - Closing Meeting – Agenda & Attendees
  - Auditor Notes
  - Audit Work Papers

There is no specified format since audits take many forms. It can be hard copy or soft copy but regardless of format it must be uploaded and stored in the Digital Action Tracking System. It is acceptable to retain Auditor Notes and Work Papers in hard copy form as long as they are referenced and stored for at least five years. Closure of all Action Items from the audit shall be documented in Action Tracking System.

## 5. Roles & Responsibilities {#np-04-1-5-roles-and-responsibilities}

### SSHQE Manager/Audit Owner {#np-04-1-5-sshqe-manager-audit-owner}
- Identify the risks associated with a specific set of SSHQE requirements and assess the internal audits or third-party audits required to determine compliance with these requirements
- Plan audits and reviews in the assigned areas of responsibility
- Establish budget plans and human resources necessary for internal SSHQE audits
- Selection of the audit or review Audit Team leader / Lead Auditor
- Follow-up and tracking of audit and review findings
- Approve the Audit Schedule as developed by the Audit Team Leader

### Audit Team Leader / Lead Auditor {#np-04-1-5-audit-team-leader}
- IRCA Certified or Internal auditor certified with minimum 2 years’ experience
- Plan the audit requirements, Audit schedule and arrangements with the Auditee, establish and train the audit team
- Issue of the Audit Notification and schedule
- Leads the Opening and Closing Meetings
- Leads and facilitates all audit or review activities and conducts audit and review meetings. The Leader may choose to delegate certain of these activities to qualified Members.
- Assure the ongoing independence and objectivity of the team members and the audit report
- Records, collates and present findings, including all Objective Evidence
- With support of the Auditee, determines Observations and appropriate actions
- Prepares and issues draft and final audit reports
- Monitors and tracks actions, follow-ups and closes-out as appropriate and feeds into the management review process.
- Assures all findings are entered into the Digital Action Tracking System (DATS) and are closed out in a timely manner

### Audit Team Member {#np-04-1-5-audit-team-member}
- Supports the Audit Team Leader / Lead Auditor in all audit activities
- Carries out audit activities and records all relevant findings in accordance with audit procedure
- Participate in audit training recommended by the Audit Team Leader
- Works under the direction of the Audit Team Leader while supporting the audit team.
- Notifies the Audit Team Leader of issues involving independence and objectivity

### Auditee / Manager Being Audited / Representative {#np-04-1-5-auditee}
Regarding audits within the Manager’s area of responsibility:
- Insuring his/her staff supports the audit effort
- Work with the Audit Owner and the Audit Team Leader to comply with this audit procedure and the audit plan as defined.
- Ensure the reasonable availability of plant facilities required for execution of the audit in accordance with the approved Audit Schedule
- Cooperate with the Audit Team Leader to develop acceptable findings based on the evidence provided and implement corrective actions as appropriate to effectively address the audit findings
- Receive audit reports and determine their distribution and appointing responsible parties within his/her organization
- Work with the Audit Team Leader to develop clear and effective Corrective Actions based on the Objective Evidence presented by the Audit Team.
- Determine work plans and priorities for Corrective Actions
- Follows up actions and liaise with the Audit Team Leader to close out findings in a timely manner

### SSHQE Officer {#np-04-1-5-sshqe-officer}
- Assist Audit Team Leader to prepare the TOR work plan and Corrective Actions
- Assist SSHQE Performance Administration to record the audit findings in the Digital Action Tracking System (DATS)
- Maintain ISO 9001 / 14001 / 45001 documents of the internal audit record
- Monitor progress of corrective actions in support of the Audit Team Leader
- Record the audit result in the PT. LNG Action Tracking System
- Monitor progress of corrective actions in support of the Audit Team Leader

# Chapter 2: External Audit {#np-04-2-external-audit}

## External Audit Procedure {#np-04-2-external-audit-procedure}

| Procedure | PIC | Remarks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Establishment of plan | HSE OFFICER | Prepare the next year’s assessment plan in Dec. of every year. Intervals of audit follow the rules of certification body. Organization form: “(NP04-01) Audit program”. Assessment application should be done before 30 days of planned assessment day to certification body. |
| Preparation of assessment | HSE OFFICER & Auditee | In the event of an assessment, the HSE OFFICER should make preparation in prior consultation with the certification body about the schedule, etc. Lest the assessment should be delayed. The team to be audited should co-operate so that the planned assessment may progress smoothly. |
| Notification of assessment | HSE OFFICER | When receiving an assessment notification from the certification body, notify the same fact at once to the team to be audited. |
| Assessment performance | Auditee / Team | The assessment should progress in accordance with the rules of the certification body. Its performance is corresponding with procedures for the internal audit. |
| Report of audit results | Auditee & HSE OFFICER | Confirm non-conformities found by the assessor, sign the “report of non-conformities” of the certification body, and agree on the estimated date of correction date. (the period of correction action: within 3 months). The estimated date of correction action means the date when the “report of non-conformity” reaches the certification body. As to the “Report of Non-conformity”, the auditee should keep its copy, & its original should be sent to the HSE OFFICER. For NC which were found, in principle, the auditee should take corrective action on them. But as to NC related to another team, the SM should specify which team should take corrective action, & make the team take the corrective action. The team who takes corrective action should finish the corrective action in 3 months. He should send the “Report of Non-conformity” in which the result is entered together with documented information evidence to the SM (HSE OFFICER). The HSE OFFICER review the NC which took corrective action. In the event that the result is judged to be satisfactory, he must enter the contents of corrective action in the original of the “Report of Non-conformity” of the certification body, and send it to the certification body approved it by the SM. But the corrective action is not satisfactory, he should send back the “Report”, and make it take re-corrective action. The SM must deal with the certification body’s reply: If satisfactory, notify the completion of the audit to the auditee. If not satisfactory, take re-corrective action according to assessor’s notes. |

## Division of Non-Conformity by the Certification Body {#np-04-2-division-of-non-conformity}

### Major Non-Conformity {#np-04-2-major-non-conformity}

The major non-conformity which is considered to seriously threaten safety at sea, safety of life, or the marine environment, and must be corrected immediately with corrective action. Or, if there is combination of such non-conformities, for example:
- Violation of procedures prescribed by compulsory rules and regulations.
- In the event that corrective action was not performed on non-conformity which had been in the previous audit in connection with safety and pollution prevention.
- In the event that the SM avoided responsibility relevant to safety & pollution prevention.

### Minor Non-Conformity {#np-04-2-minor-non-conformity}

The minor non-conformity refers to a case which objective evidence that does not meet a specific requirement of ISO or of the certification body rules of the certification body was observed, for example:
- In the event that written procedures were not carried out.
- In the event that a code, an instruction, a standard, etc. which are not compulsory but disagree.
- In the event that training, etc, was not conducted in the prescribed manner.
- Lack of education requiring familiarization in preparation for accidents caused by human error.
