# Chapter 1. Contractor Safety Management System Procedure {#np-06-1-csms-procedure}

## 1. Purpose {#np-06-1-1-purpose}

Contractors and other third parties who provide materials and services on behalf of **LNG Nias Gasification** (**PT. LNG**) have a significant effect on our operations. It is therefore essential that these services be held to standards consistent with all **PT. LNG** policies and objectives. The Contractor Safety Management System (CSMS) provides the work processes and requirements to assist the **PT. LNG** personnel in meeting these obligations.

LNG Nias Gasification is committed to conducting business in a manner that protects the safety and health of employees, its contractors, its customers, and the public in its operations. Element 3 of the **PT. LNG** HSE Management System and Element 6 of **PT. LNG** Process Safety Management Plan requires that a system be in place to assure:
* A system is in place for evaluation, selection, and ongoing assessment of critical material vendors and service contractors that includes an assessment of their capabilities to perform services or provide materials in accordance with HSE requirements.
* A system is in place to periodically assess the performance of critical material vendors and service contractors.
* Performance feedback is provided to these contractors to promote continuous improvement.

The goal of this procedure is to achieve the requirements of Element 3 of the **PT. LNG** HSE Management System concerning contractor management. The purpose of this procedure is to ensure that contractors perform their work in compliance with applicable **PT. LNG** quality, health, safety, and environmental performance standards and requirements.


## 2. Scope {#np-06-1-2-scope}

### In Scope {#np-06-1-2-1-in-scope}
This Procedure is applied to all service contractors who perform work under **PT. LNG** premises.

### Out of Scope {#np-06-1-2-2-out-of-scope}
This procedure does not apply to suppliers, vendors, or contractors' facilities, systems, or organizations that are not directly associated with **PT. LNG** activities.

* **Contract Owner**:
  * Understand there is a procedure in place that must be followed when issuing any contract and that all contractors are subject to this procedure.

  * To conduct initial risk assessment by completing the Work Contract Severity Assessment form.

  * Ensure work activity in their department that involves a contractor complies with requirements of this procedure.

  * Identify potential contractors for site work.
  * Inform the HSE team when the contractor HSE Pre-Qualification review is required.
  * Issue bid packages for contractors and arranges for pre-bid meeting as necessary.
  * Select contractor with input, in terms of relevant matters, from the HSE team as needed.
  * Issue contracts in accordance with PT. LNG Procurement Policy and Procedures.
  * Support the Vendor Management System as required by PT. LNG Procurement Policy and Procedures.
  * Assign PCD staff to maintain the performance record based on the final audit result.

  * Develop annual CSMS audit plan.
  * Maintain audit record as tracking action.
  * Follow up close out action to contractor.
  * Provide an appropriate level of training to personnel regarding the requirements of the Contractor Safety Management System.

  * Maintain CSMS forms, practices & procedures related to HSE matters and assure they are available to personnel required to use them.


## 6. Procedure Section {#np-06-1-6-procedure-section}

### 6.1 The CSMS Process {#np-06-1-6-1-the-csms-process}
The Contractor Safety Management System is a risk-based approach to ensure compliance with the PT. LNG HSE Management System. The application of CSMS depends on the type of work to be performed and the potential risks associated with the work in terms of the potential for impact to employees, contract workers, customers, the environment, and the community. This potential for harm is determined by PT. LNG personnel and is based on the scope of the proposed work. Consequently, the greater the potential for harm, the greater the scrutiny and control demanded by the CSMS.

### 6.2 Overview of CSMS {#np-06-1-6-2-overview-of-csms}
The first step to take once it is determined that use of a contractor is necessary is to identify the risks of the work to be performed. Factors to consider include:
* Nature of work to be performed.
* Location of the work to be performed, e.g., in a process area or non-process area.
* Potential for the contractor's work to impact process safety.
* Potential for the contractor's work to result in life-threatening injuries/illnesses to contractor or company employees.
* Potential for the contractor's work to result in exposure to significant personnel safety or environmental risks.
* Potential exposure to publicity.

Based on the evaluation score performed by PT. LNG staff, the contractor's work is classified as one of three broad risk categories below:
1. High Risk Potential
2. Medium Risk Potential
3. Low Risk Potential

Once the contract has potential for a catastrophic incident, then the category will be set on high risk directly. If there is doubt regarding the impact of the hazard or the effectiveness of proposed mitigation measures, the risk category should be raised to the next higher level. The assigned Risk Level can change at any time during the evaluation or execution of the work.

### 6.3 Classification of Contractor Risks {#np-06-1-6-3-classification-of-contractor-risks}

| Question | Decision / Classification |
| --- | --- |
| Does the work involve maintenance, repair or renovation of process equipment? Does the work involves any component covered by the Process Safety Management (PSM) Program? Could the work potentially result in a High (Orange) or Extreme (Red) Severity Code Incident as defined in the QHSSE Risk Assessment Matrix? | Yes -> **Category 1: High Risk** |
| Could the work involve non-maintenance services that could impact process safety? Could the work result in life-threatening injuries or illnesses to contractor or DSLNG personnel? Could the work result in exposure to a significant personnel safety issue or a significant environmental risk? | Yes -> **Category 2: Medium Risk** |
| Could the work potentially – as a worst case – result in a Medium (Yellow) Severity Code Incident as defined in the QHSSE Risk Assessment Matrix? | No -> **Category 3: Low Risk** (Work could potentially result in only a Low (Green) Severity Code Incident) |

### 6.4 Differentiation Of Monitoring Based on Risk Assessment {#np-06-1-6-4-differentiation-of-monitoring}

| Work Process Step | Category 1 (Low) | Category 2 (Medium) | Category 3 (High) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-Qualification: Initial Risk Assessment | Required (HSE Policy Only) | Required (CSMS Document Review) | Required (CSMS Document Review and site verification) |
| Evaluation & Selection: HSE Questionnaire | Required (HSE Policy Only) | Required (CSMS Document Review) | Required (CSMS Document Review and site verification) |
| Pre-Job Preparation: Set HSE KPI | Discretionary | Required | Required |
| Execution Monitoring: Regular Audit | Discretionary | Required (Depending on contract period and POB) | Required (Depending on contract period and POB) |
| Demobilization: Performance Review | Discretionary | Discretionary | Required (For construction project based more than 1 year) |

### 6.5 CSMS Work Process {#np-06-1-6-5-csms-work-process}
There are five phases of the contractor management process:
1. Pre-Qualification Phase
2. Evaluation & Selection Phase
3. Pre-Job Preparation Phase
4. Execution & Monitoring Phase
5. Demobilization / Contractor Performance Review

#### 6.5.1 Pre-Qualification Phase {#np-06-1-6-5-1-pre-qualification-phase}
Contractors rendering services in any classification of risk must be pre-qualified. The CSMS Pre-Qualification phase must precede any bidding activities. Only contractors that pass or are exempted from the CSMS Pre-Qualification will be included into the bidder list for further bidding process.

For low-risk contract, HSE Policy review as part of pre-qualification is mandatory.
For medium and high-risk contract, contractors must submit complete CSMS document containing:
1. Leadership and commitment.
2. Policy and strategic HSE objectives.
3. Organization, responsibility, resources, standards and documentation.
4. Hazards management.
5. Planning and procedures.
6. Performance monitoring.
7. Audit and review process.
8. Emergency management.
9. Continuous improvement.

Exemptions from complete CSMS document pre-qualification for medium and high-risk contracts:
* The proposed services are classified as Category 1 (Low Risk), or
* Within the past year the contractor has either successfully passed a HSE Pre-Qualification for similar work (even if the contract was not awarded), or
* The contractor is currently performing PT. LNG work in a satisfactory manner or has received a Satisfactory Final Evaluation for completion of similar work.

Pre-qualification certificates awarded are valid for 2 (two) years, but may be reviewed or cancelled if:
* Changes in PT. LNG Procurement Management process / procedure impact prequalification.
* Contractor's organization or business scope changes (merger, acquisition, etc.).
* Contractor has a significant violation of government regulation.
* Contractor violates code of conduct rules during service.
* Contractor has a poor assessment result / unsatisfactory service from another site/project.

#### 6.5.2 Evaluation & Selection Phase {#np-06-1-6-5-2-evaluation-and-selection-phase}
Contractors are selected based on commercial, technical, and HSE aspects of the bid. For medium and high-risk contract, decisions are made by assessing documents, clarification meetings, and site/facility inspections.
Evaluation metrics include:
* Responses regarding the HSE Questionnaire
* Performance on Previous Similar Jobs: Recordable Incident Rate (RIF), OSHA Day Away from Work Case Rate (DAFWC), Fatalities
* Documentation of proposed HSE Program
* Assessment of ability to meet Site HSE performance standards, General HSE requirements, and English proficiency (speaking and reading)

The General Services and Support Manager shall approve award recommendations and inform bidders.

#### 6.5.3 Pre-Job Preparation Phase {#np-06-1-6-5-3-pre-job-preparation-phase}
An HSE plan is required for:
* Any contract with more than 1 year duration and minimum 10 POB on site, or
* High-risk project less than 1 year period with more than 50 personnel on board (POB), or
* As per QHSE Manager discretion.

The HSE plan must be developed and signed by Contractor HSE representative and Project Manager, reviewed and approved by PT. LNG QHSE Manager and Contract Owner.
Minimum content covers: Sign Sheet Cover, Work Scope, Leadership & Commitment, Policy, Strategic Objectives and KPI, Organization, responsibilities, resource, standards, and documentation, Legal Compliance, Evaluation & Risk Management, Training & Competence, Planning & procedures, Equipment, Tools and Materials, Implementation & Performance Monitoring, Auditing & Review, Continuous Improvement, HSE Program.

##### Pre-Job Meeting {#np-06-1-6-5-3-1-pre-job-meeting}
Contract Owner is responsible for holding a Pre-Job Meeting/kick-off meeting to convey PT. LNG expectations on technical and non-technical issues and ensure HSE issues are covered.

##### Pre-Job Documentation {#np-06-1-6-5-3-2-pre-job-documentation}
Required documentation prior to mobilization:
* Agreed HSE KPI.
* Specific method statement related to main job.
* Finalized HSE Plan.
* Approved HSE program.
* List of portable equipment/tools brought to site and integrity verifications.
* Inspection records and certificates for lifting equipment (cranes, forklifts).
* MIGAS Certificates for heavy equipment operators.
* Electrical generating equipment integrity verification by certified electrician.
* Name plate verification for ASME pressure vessels.
* Craft skills professional certification for scaffolding, electrical, rigging, mechanics, welding, pipefitting, instrumentation.
* Verification that subcontractors are on proposal and HSE Qualified list.

#### 6.5.4 Execution Monitoring Phase {#np-06-1-6-5-4-execution-monitoring-phase}
A periodical CSMS audit is conducted yearly for contractors meeting:
1. Risk category: medium or high.
2. Duration of service: more than 1 year.
3. Personnel on board: more than 10 POB.

Reviews are conducted using Job Inspection and Execution Monitoring Checklist.

#### 6.5.5 Contractor Final / Annual Performance Review {#np-06-1-6-5-5-performance-review}
A final performance review is required for project contractors building new facilities (>1 year) and other service contracts to assess overall HSE performance.

Outcome options for QHSE Manager:
1. Recommend contractor remain on "Approved Bidders List" in Vendor Management Database.
2. Place contractor on probation.
3. Recommend dismissal.

##### Probation {#np-06-1-6-5-5-1-probation}
Placed on probation by Contractor Review Committee (CRC) if failing site HSE standards. Contractor must investigate causes, submit action plan, and submit quarterly status reports for 12 months.

##### Reinstatement Criteria {#np-06-1-6-5-5-2-reinstatement-criteria}
Dismissed contractors may apply for reinstatement after:
* Waiting minimum period set by CRC.
* Submitting written document demonstrating corrected deficiencies.
* Completing satisfactory audit of health and safety programs at other sites.
* Obtaining written approval from CRC.

##### Lessons Learned {#np-06-1-6-5-5-3-lessons-learned}
Documented by PCD to improve contractor performance and selection.

# Chapter 2. Requisition and Supply {#np-06-2-requisition-and-supply}

## 1. Requisition {#np-06-2-1-requisition}

Before submitting a requisition, the Site Manager should check the inventory and record it accurately so as to avoid a requisition of unnecessary goods.
All items required for machinery maintenance or onsite repair should be discussed with the SM before making a requisition.
Site manager should dispatch the stores/spare parts Requisition (NP06-07, 08) to TECHNICAL/LOG in company (Nias Site) prior to 7 days than the estimated date of supply by FAX, E-Mail or post mail. And 1 month than the estimated date of supply for Periodic Stores Supply.

Only periodical supply for on site's stores shall be allowed. But excluding items are lubricant oils, chemical & gases which are possible to request any time.

### How to request onsite stores {#np-06-2-1-1-how-to-request-onsite-stores}
'On site's store Requisition (NP06-07)' should be as below:
* General stores
* Spare parts equipment
* Paint
* Safety equipment appliances
* Medicines
* Material for repair
* Lubricating Oil
* Chemicals & Gases

The No. of ISSA/IMPA Code should be entered whenever possible. The requisition for on site's store is separated by the above major items.

### How to request spare parts {#np-06-2-1-2-how-to-request-spare-parts}
Requested by the 'Spare part Requisition (NP06-08)' and separated with "Requisition form" per each machinery.
When requesting spare parts, the requisition should be made for a proper quantity in Consideration of necessaries and minimum quantity on the basis of consumption record, present quantity on board, maintenance plan and machinery condition.
Emergency items should be marked with "Urgent" on the requisition sheet.
For items different from the drawing or remodeled after initial construction, their dimensions should be accurately measured and entered in the requisition.


## 2. Purchase & Supply {#np-06-2-2-purchase-and-supply}

### 2.1 Review and Selection of Reasonableness of Purchase {#np-06-2-2-1-review-of-purchase}
Upon receipt of a requisition sheet from an onsite, LOG & TECHNICAL should determine the proper quantity of supply after discussion with the site manager or responsible officer considering:
* Characteristics of the onsite
* Recent supply, remains on site & standard quantity of spare stores required by the flag
* Validity period (safety appliances, etc)
* On site's machinery status report, onsite work report and plan
* Budget and urgency

TECHNICAL should prepare a list of items which standard quantity is set out by law, regulation or convention, and use it as reference for evaluation.

### 2.2 Request for Quotations {#np-06-2-2-2-request-for-quotations}
After TECHNICAL /LOG review requisitions and select potential supplier, TECHNICAL /LOG request quotation to supplier or Maker.
Request for quotation should be sent to at least three (3) suppliers, except:
1. Direct purchase from the maker
2. Items under a long-term supply contract
3. Items of monopoly
4. Urgently requested items for which multiple tenders cannot be obtained
5. Subcontractor presenting reasonable prices through repeated deals

If point 1-5 have not been met, quotation still prepared by approval from SMD HEAD.

### 2.3 Review of Price, Approval and Order {#np-06-2-2-3-review-and-order}
Reasonable price estimated on basis of relative prices, purchase history, market price, etc.
Person in charge for onsite stores should approve by SMD HEAD with following documents:
* On site's requisition
* Quotation
* Budget and consumption after supply reported to SMD HEAD.

Information to supplier:
* On site's name, Place and date of supply
* Items, quantity and unit price (Accurate part number and specifications; certificate requirements if needed)

Supplier should supply onsite store in bulk to reduce packaging volumes, encouraging recycling initiatives and using non-disposable equipment.

### 2.4 Notice to Onsite {#np-06-2-2-4-notice-to-onsite}
Details of supply informed to onsite. If supply delayed, TECHNICAL informs delayed reason to site.


## 3. SUPPLY of Fuel Oil {#np-06-2-3-supply-of-fuel-oil}

Operational and Bunker Team of onsite-owner is in charge of fuel oil supply. When receiving on site's requisition for fuel oil, check oil spec, expected consumption amount and remaining amount, discuss with TECHNICAL, decide and notice spec, amount, place and date of supply to site manager.


# Chapter 3. Subcontractor Management {#np-06-3-subcontractor-management}

## 1. Selection of Subcontractor {#np-06-3-1-selection-of-subcontractor}

### 1.1 Identification of Necessity {#np-06-3-1-1-identification-of-necessity}
ONSITE Head shall identify necessity of subcontractor based on:
* Possibility of reduction of manpower and expenses through "Long-term supply contract"
* Possibility of providing good service and goods
* Possibility of continual technical development support
* Possibility of consistent and economical purchase and supply

### 1.2 Collection and Analysis of Subcontractor Status {#np-06-3-1-2-collection-of-status}
ONSITE shall collect and analyze data using:
* Collection of Organization Description, Balance Sheet of Previous Year, Tariff, etc.
* Assessment for registered subcontractor for existing subcontractor
* Inquiry of same kind of subcontractor
* Data collection from mass media or specialized organization
* Recommendation of related team

### 1.3 Selection Process {#np-06-3-1-3-selection-process}
ONSITE fills Assessment of subcontractor and takes approval from Site Manager. Site Manager selects subcontractor after review (holding temporary management meeting if needed). Selected subcontractor requires approval of top management representative.


## 2. Subcontractor Management and Assessment {#np-06-3-2-management-and-assessment}

### 2.1 Subcontractor Management {#np-06-3-2-1-subcontractor-management}
In case of non-conforming supply/sub-standard service or dissatisfaction from SM/customer, team leader identifies non-conformity and handles according to NP-06. NC reports used as assessment data.

### 2.2 Subcontractor Assessments {#np-06-3-2-2-subcontractor-assessments}
Assessed around end of Dec. annually. Subcontractors without supply record during last several years are skipped.
Temporary assessment conducted if critical defects occur or critical reason identified. Site Manager reviews Assessment for Registered Subcontractor.


## 3. Re-registration and Cancellation {#np-06-3-3-re-registration-and-cancellation}

Subcontractor registration cancelled if:
* Critical defects occur in products/services provided
* Result of periodical assessment is below 60 points
* Management identifies reason for cancellation

ONSITE draws subcontractor canceling reason 'free form', reports to ONSITE Head, and takes approval.


# Chapter 4. Receiving and Tallying {#np-06-4-receiving-and-tallying}

## 1. Receiving and Tallying {#np-06-4-1-receiving-and-tallying}

Person in charge of tallying is Site Manager (if absent, Sr. Operating Team Leader assumes duty, confirmed by SM later).
Tally person must attend supply site and confirm specification, quality, size match requisition and invoice.

### Standard of Tallying {#np-06-4-1-1-standard-of-tallying}
Basis for tallying:
* Conformity to specification, invoice or requisition
* Any damage, spoilage, or loss
* Other non-conformity

Visual inspection or measurement report performed against invoice, order sheet, or drawings. For quantity of same item >= 10, sampling within 20-50% permitted.


## 2. Loading of Site's Stores {#np-06-4-2-loading-stores}

Stores passing tallying inspection loaded on Site. Whole crew cooperates for loading in shortest time.
When received in good condition, SM/Dept SM checks item/quantities on voucher, puts name, rank, Site stamp, date, and signs.


## 3. Actions for Missing or Non-Conforming Supply {#np-06-4-3-missing-or-nonconforming}

Missing items reported on 'Receiving/Tally Report (NP06-17)' and sent to LOG (except informed non-supplies).
Substandard goods returned immediately or replacement requested; unreturned/unreplaced recorded in NP06-17. Urgent unreplaced items reported to LOG immediately.


## 4. Receiving Report {#np-06-4-4-receiving-report}

NP06-17 forwarded to LOG prior to departure from supply port. Prepared in duplicate per Dept (1 kept on Site, 1 to LOG). Prepared even if delivery through agent/superintendent, for whole supply.


## 5. Supply, Tally and Protest of Fuel Oil {#np-06-4-5-fuel-oil-supply}

Safe bunker supply per "Guidance for Environmental Protection".

### Inspections of Quantity and Protest {#np-06-4-5-1-inspections-and-protest}
* Sounding tank water test with water checker; sampling at manifold.
* If supplied amount differs from delivery receipt, SM publishes protest letter and submits to MET and Site-owner.
* Actual received amount recorded in remark column on delivery receipt or free-form protest letter drawn up with supplier signature.
* Statement of facts attached.

### Sampling and Reporting {#np-06-4-5-2-sampling-and-reporting}
* Delivery receipt/order retained >= 3 years; sample retained >= 12 months.
* Bunker quantity reported to Site owner and Technical. Log book vs actual difference reported immediately.


# Chapter 5. Controlling Inventory {#np-06-5-controlling-inventory}

## 1. Responsibility and Authority {#np-06-5-1-responsibility-and-authority}

* **Site Manager (SM)**: Overall responsibility for control of Site stores, supervises economic inventory control, settles disputes between departments, supervises spare parts/repair material requisition and inventory control.
* **Chief of Department / Chief Engineer**: Maintains proper inventory quantity safely, requests accurate supply, attends supply/tally, trains crew on correct use, maintains neat storage arrangement, prepares inventory reports, discusses with other dept to avoid dual requisition.


## 2. Storage {#np-06-5-2-storage}

### 2.1 General Site Stores {#np-06-5-2-1-general-stores}
Stored at designated places per dept. Items subject to damage/degeneration specially controlled.

### 2.2 Safety Appliances {#np-06-5-2-2-safety-appliances}
Stored at designated places separately. Checked every voyage, replaced before expiry.

### 2.3 Paint {#np-06-5-2-3-paint}
Stored in warehouses equipped with fire extinguishers. Cans turned frequently to avoid precipitation.

### 2.4 Wire Rope and Cargo Securing Devices {#np-06-5-2-4-wire-rope}
Stored in dry place free from sea water after greasing.

### 2.5 Chart and Publication {#np-06-5-2-5-charts}
Stored in the bridge.

### 2.6 Spare Parts {#np-06-5-2-6-spare-parts-storage}
Stored in spare part box in E/R or designated place, preferably in separate box with tags. Emergency/long-term unused items tagged with name/usage, greased or covered in vinyl sheet.


## 3. Control at Site {#np-06-5-3-control-at-site}

### 3.1 Stores Control {#np-06-5-3-1-stores-control}
Chief of each dept controls receipt and disbursement under custody.

### 3.2 Secondhand Goods and Unused Material {#np-06-5-3-2-secondhand-goods}
* **Secondhand Goods**: Separated into usable (greased/stored as emergency spares) and repairable (maintenance requisition made to TECHNICAL with dept chief opinion).
* **Application for Disposal of Material Not in Use**: Replaced items collected, 'Application for Disposal of Material Not in Use' (free form) submitted to TECHNICAL. Non-polluting consumables beyond repair disposed by SM and reported later.
* **Shore Transfer**: Transferred ashore at domestic port calls as determined by TECHNICAL.

### 3.3 Inventory List (NP06-09) Management {#np-06-5-3-3-inventory-list-management}
Maintained/updated immediately upon stock change (supply, use, loss, damage, wear, return).
Distinguishes between New and Old quantities (except engine valves without distinction; unusable excluded).
SM submits 'Inventory List (NP06-09)' in **May & November** and 'Important Spare Part List (NP06-10)' **quarterly** to organization. Responsible person: Chief Engineer.

Inventory list includes:
* Machinery name, spare part name and description
* Standard quantity
* Quantity in use
* Stock quantity at end of last year
* Used quantity, Received quantity, Present stock quantity

### 3.4 Medicine Control {#np-06-5-3-4-medicine-control}
SM appoints Medical Officer (standard ISO qualified; if none, SM assumes duties).
Medical Officer duties:
* Provide treatment/medication, report injuries/illnesses and daily condition to SM.
* Request medical advice/assistance via Radio Medical Centres/coastal stations.
* Record ALL treatment in Medical Logbook (date, patient name/rank, condition/symptoms, treatment details).
* Maintain medical locker per International Medical Guide for Sites and Flag State Regulations.
* Check/replenish first aid kits (including lifeboats).
* Maintain drug expiry dates and order replacements in good time.
* Land time-expired drugs to Port Medical Authorities and obtain receipt.
* Update medicines monthly and submit **Medicine Control List every 6 months**.

### 3.5 Controlled Drugs {#np-06-5-3-5-controlled-drugs}
Responsible: SM.
Included drugs:
* Codeine Sulphate tablets
* Diazepam injection & tablets
* Morphine sulphate injection
* Phenobarbital tablets

Procedure: Locked in Site safe/secure place in SM accommodation. Issued ONLY by SM. Inventory retained by SM. All issues recorded in Medical Logbook. Expired drugs landed ashore with signed/dated receipt.

### 3.6 Excluded Items {#np-06-5-3-6-excluded-items}
One-time consumable items excluded from inventory checking.

# Chapter 6. Vendor And Subcontractor Maintenance Procedures {#np-06-6-vendor-subcontractor-maintenance}

## 1. Purpose {#np-06-6-1-purpose}

This procedure is designed to:
* Establish official guidelines for managing vendors and subcontractors to ensure they meet quality, cost, time, and health, safety, and environmental (HSE) standards.
* Ensure all vendors and subcontractors working for the company are selected transparently, evaluated regularly, and managed systematically.
* Provide a reference for all departments interacting with vendors/subcontractors at every stage of work.


## 2. Scope {#np-06-6-2-scope}

* Applies to all procurement activities involving third parties (materials, maintenance, construction, consulting, field subcontractors).
* Applies to all company locations (head office, projects, operational facilities).
* Does not apply to small transactions below threshold (e.g. direct purchases < Rp 5 million) unless specified.


## 3. References {#np-06-6-3-references}

| Document Type | Document Title |
| --- | --- |
| ISO 9001:2015 | Quality Management System |
| ISO 14001:2015 | Environmental Management System |

| UU No. 13 Tahun 2003 | Indonesian Labour Law |


## 4. Definitions {#np-06-6-4-definitions}

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Vendor | Goods/service providers who have direct contracts with the company |
| Subcontractor | A third party that carries out part of the scope of work of the main contractor |
| Pre-Qualification (PQ) | The initial assessment process of vendors/subcontractors before tender |
| Service Level Agreement (SLA) | An agreement that sets out performance standards vendor must meet |
| KPI | Key Performance Indicator for measuring vendor/subcontractor success |
| Permit to Work (PTW) | Official work permit before carrying out work at company location |
| Non-Conformity Report (NCR) | Report of non-conformities found in vendor's work execution |
| Approved Vendor List (AVL) | List of approved vendors/subcontractors based on evaluation |


## 5. Leadership & Responsibilities {#np-06-6-5-leadership-responsibilities}

* **Top Management**: Approve policies, provide resources, final decisions on strategic selection, approve AVL, handle escalated issues.
* **Procurement Department**: Pre-qualification, tender, evaluation, draft contracts, maintain database, monitor administrative compliance, coordinate annual evaluations.
* **User Department (Operations/Engineering/Project)**: Oversee field implementation, report daily performance/issues, KPI inputs, technical coordination.
* **SSHQE Department**: Ensure OHS compliance, safety induction, OHS audits, prepare NCRs for violations.
* **Finance Department**: Process payments based on progress, ensure payments align with SLA/contract, evaluate vendor financial capacity.

## 7. Contract Management {#np-06-6-7-contract-management}

### 7.1 Contract Lifecycle {#np-06-6-7-1-contract-lifecycle}
Stages: Contract Drafting -> Negotiation -> Contract Signing -> Implementation -> Monitoring & Control -> Amendment (if necessary) -> Contract Closure.

### 7.2 Standard Contract Structure {#np-06-6-7-2-contract-structure}
Must include: Preamble, Definitions, Scope of Work (SOW), Schedule, Deliverables, Payment Terms, Obligations of Vendor/Subcontractor, Obligations of Company, HSE Clause, Quality Requirements, Confidentiality Clause, Force Majeure, Termination, Penalties, Governing Law.

### 7.3 Service Level Agreement (SLA) {#np-06-6-7-3-sla}

| Parameter | SLA Target | Evaluation Method | Penalty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Response to emergency damage | < 2 hours | Work report log | IDR 2,000,000 per delay |
| Completion of minor work | Max 3 working days | Progress report | 5% deduction from work value |
| Availability of onsite personnel | 95% of schedule | Attendance & timesheet | Warning letter and penalty |
| HSE Compliance | 100% Zero Accident | HSE Audit | Blacklist in case of LTI |

### 7.4 Health, Safety, Environment Clause {#np-06-6-7-4-hse-clause}
Mandatory clauses: Comply with company K3L regulations, undergo safety induction, provide PPE, responsible for accidents from negligence, company reserves right to terminate work for serious violations.

### 7.5 Contract Monitoring {#np-06-6-7-5-contract-monitoring}
Conducted via monthly progress reports, coordination meetings, site inspections, performance evaluations.

### 7.6 Contract Amendment & Closure {#np-06-6-7-6-amendment-closure}
Amendments require signed Addendum. Closure requires final handover, submission of as-built drawings/final report/certificates, completion of all payments, and entering evaluation results into database.


## 8. Vendor Performance Monitoring & Evaluation {#np-06-6-8-performance-monitoring}

### 8.1 Monitoring Mechanism {#np-06-6-8-1-monitoring-mechanism}
* **Daily**: Field reports and timesheets
* **Monthly**: Progress reports and review meetings
* **Quarterly**: SLA compliance audits
* **Annually**: Comprehensive evaluation

### 8.2 Key Performance Indicators (KPI) & Scoring {#np-06-6-8-2-kpi-scoring}

| Category | Indicator | Weight | Assessment Method |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quality | Products/services according to specs, defect rate | 30% | Inspection, NCR report |
| Delivery | Completion of work on time | 25% | Schedule vs realization |
| Cost Control | Consistency on contract prices, cost efficiency | 15% | Audit Finance |
| HSE | Zero accident, PPE compliance, incident reports | 20% | HSE audit, accident report |
| Support & Communication | Responsiveness, communication, documentation | 10% | Feedback User |

Scoring: (5) Excellent, (4) Good, (3) Fair, (2) Poor, (1) Very Poor. Formula: Final Score = Sum(Score x Weight).

### 8.3 Classification of Evaluation Results {#np-06-6-8-3-classification-results}

| Score % | Category | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| >= 85% | Excellent | Prioritized for next contract |
| 70–84% | Good | Remain on Approved Vendor List |
| 50–69% | Fair | Strict supervision, need improvement |
| < 50% | Poor | Blacklisted and no longer recommended |

## 12. Improvement & Corrective Actions {#np-06-6-12-improvement-corrective-actions}

### 12.1 Classification of Nonconformities {#np-06-6-12-1-nonconformity-types}
* **Major Nonconformity**: Serious failure impacting safety, environment, or quality (e.g. fatal accident, forged documents).
* **Minor Nonconformity**: Minor deviation without immediate major impact (e.g. late report, incomplete PPE).
* **Observation**: Potential problem not yet having impact (e.g. poor housekeeping).

### 12.2 Corrective Action Flow {#np-06-6-12-2-corrective-action-flow}
Identification -> Reporting (NCR) -> Root Cause Analysis (RCA) -> Corrective Action Plan (CAP) -> Implementation -> Verification -> Closure.

### Requisition Formula {#np-06-form-np06-24-formula}
* Format: `[Code]-[Category Code]-[Year Code][Req No]`
* Year Code: 2025 -> 25 | 2026 -> 26
* Req No: 001 ~ 999
* Date of Requisition: YYYY-MM-DD
* Examples:
  * `GEN-GST-25001` (General Consumables)
  * `SAF-CHEM-25002` (Environmental Chemicals)
  * `SAF-APD-25003` (PPE)
  * `GEN-COM-25004` (Communication Equipment)
  * `SAF-EMG-25005` (Emergency Equipment)
  * `MRO-SPR-25006` (Spare Parts & Repair)
